# Selected product leadership case studies

These abbreviated cases focus on the product judgment and operating patterns behind the work. They use verified, public-safe claims and intentionally omit confidential implementation detail.

## 1. Architecting a 0-to-1 creator-commerce platform

**Situation**

A new Brand Platform needed to unify fragmented creator discovery, campaign, reporting, communication, payment, and attribution workflows while supporting a move toward performance-based economics.

**My role**

I served as product architect: leading direct customer discovery, facilitating story mapping and concept validation, making reuse-versus-build decisions, and connecting workflows across Product, Design, Engineering, Data, and Commercial partners. I stayed engaged through testing, QA, and launch.

**Product judgment**

- Treat the product as one end-to-end operating system rather than a collection of feature requests.
- Reuse proven capabilities where they shortened time to value; rebuild where the old architecture constrained the target experience.
- Pair workflow architecture with commercial-model and measurement decisions rather than deferring them until launch.

**Outcome**

The platform became the active campaign workspace for 5K+ brands.

**What this demonstrates**

0-to-1 platform strategy, multi-sided marketplace design, executive alignment, integration leadership, and accountability through the final mile.

## 2. Productizing creator-brand matching

**Situation**

Creator selection needed a better signal for brand fit and return potential than manual judgment or superficial audience attributes alone.

**My role**

As data product designer for Match.AI, I defined and refined the vectors behind the matching score and used collaborative filtering to identify creators with stronger brand fit and ROAS opportunity. I partnered with managed-service teams to validate the model before productization.

**Product judgment**

- Frame model outputs as a decision aid tied to a user workflow, not as an isolated score.
- Validate against real operating behavior before placing the model in a customer-facing product.
- Preserve an interpretable comparison and a measurable business outcome.

**Outcome**

The approach passed managed-service validation before being placed into the product experience.

**What this demonstrates**

Applied AI product design, model-to-workflow integration, human validation, data-product thinking, and outcome measurement.

## 3. Making product development agent-ready

**Situation**

Product initiatives were losing time to evolving requirements, premature date commitments, unclear ownership, hidden scope change, and context scattered across tools.

**My role**

I co-designed and operationalized a six-stage product development system from Ideation through Learn. It combined shared cross-functional ownership, explicit working agreements, measurable quality gates, post-launch learning, and localized context across Jira, GitHub, Figma, Cursor, and coding agents.

**Product judgment**

- Separate problem confidence, solution confidence, and delivery confidence.
- Commit dates only when UX fidelity and technical feasibility support them.
- Give agents bounded, source-grounded context and explicit acceptance criteria.
- Close the loop after launch so learning changes the roadmap rather than becoming a retrospective artifact.

**Outcome**

For initiatives not requiring new user research, the system reduced time from Ideation to Development start from eight weeks to four.

**What this demonstrates**

Organizational design, AI transformation, development enablement, decision quality, and a practical understanding of where agents help — and where human accountability remains essential.
