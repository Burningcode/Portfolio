# Product leadership playbooks

These playbooks turn product judgment into reusable working methods. Each is designed to help a cross-functional team make a better decision—not merely complete a template.

They are public-safe, vendor-neutral adaptations of methods I have used, taught, or operationalized. Where a playbook builds on a published framework, the original source is credited. The examples are fictional.

## Choose the playbook by the decision

| When the team needs to… | Start here | Primary output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand an ambiguous or stalled product problem | [PROBE Product Analysis](probe-product-analysis.md) | Purpose-to-experiment decision canvas |
| Translate a user problem into an end-to-end experience and releases | [Problem-to-Outcome Story Mapping](problem-to-outcome-story-mapping.md) | As-is/to-be journey, story map, and release slices |
| Make customer learning part of the weekly operating rhythm | [Continuous Discovery](continuous-discovery.md) | Evidence cadence, opportunity map, and assumption tests |
| Escape solution fixation and compare genuinely different choices | [Diverge → Converge](diverge-converge-thinking.md) | Option set, decision criteria, and learning commitments |
| Get a clear executive decision, action, or escalation | [Executive Communication](executive-communication-for-product-leaders.md) | Decision-ready narrative and read-back |
| Move a validated bet through build, launch, and learning | [Agent-Ready Product Development](agent-ready-product-development.md) | Proportional six-stage delivery contract |

## How the playbooks fit together

```text
Ambiguous situation
      ↓
Diverge → Converge: widen the frame and choose where to focus
      ↓
PROBE: connect purpose, evidence, obstacles, bets, and an experiment
      ↺
Continuous Discovery: refresh evidence and update the opportunity space
      ↓
Problem-to-Outcome Story Map: design the journey and slice end-to-end value
      ↓
Agent-Ready Product Development: build, launch, evaluate, and learn

Executive Communication supports every decision boundary.
```

This is a menu, not a mandated lifecycle. Use the smallest combination that can change the decision. A reversible product choice may need a quick PROBE and one assumption test; a cross-system bet may need the full sequence.

## Shared operating principles

Every playbook uses the same underlying contract:

1. **Name the decision.** A workshop without a decision becomes theater.
2. **Separate evidence from assumptions.** Confidence should reflect source quality, not presentation quality.
3. **Design for behavior and outcomes.** Outputs are useful only when they create observable progress.
4. **Make trade-offs and new friction visible.** Every choice changes the system for someone.
5. **Learn before irreversible commitment.** Test the assumption with the largest blast radius as cheaply as credibility allows.
6. **Preserve accountability.** Agents can gather, structure, critique, and verify; accountable people make product and release decisions.

## A lightweight working agreement

Before using any playbook, write five lines:

```text
Decision:
Owner:
Participants:
Evidence window:
Decision date:
```

Afterward, record the decision, rationale, owner, next action, and evidence that would cause the team to revisit it.
